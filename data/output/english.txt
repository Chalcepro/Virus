Simile – a comparison using “like” or “as.” Example: She is as brave as a lion.
Metaphor – a direct comparison. Example: Time is a thief.
Personification – giving human traits to non‑human things. Example: The wind whispered through the trees.
Hyperbole – deliberate exaggeration. Example: I’ve told you a million times.
Irony – when the opposite of what is expected happens. Example: A fire station burning down.
Oxymoron – combining contradictory terms. Example: deafening silence.
Alliteration – repetition of the same sound at the start of words. Example: Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.
Onomatopoeia – words that imitate sounds. Example: The bees buzzed around the hive.
Idiom – an expression whose meaning is not predictable from the usual meanings of its words. Example: It’s raining cats and dogs.
Euphemism – a mild expression used instead of a harsh one. Example: He passed away (instead of died).
Pun – a play on words. Example: I used to be a baker, but I didn’t make enough dough.
Anaphora – repetition of a word at the beginning of clauses. Example: Every day, every night, every moment, I think of you.
Metonymy – substituting the name of one thing for something related. Example: The White House issued a statement.
Synecdoche – a part used to represent the whole. Example: All hands on deck.
Apostrophe – addressing an absent person or idea. Example: O death, where is your sting?
Paradox – a statement that contradicts itself but may be true. Example: Less is more.
Litotes – understatement by using double negatives. Example: Not a bad idea.
Rhetorical question – a question asked for effect, not an answer. Example: Who doesn’t love a good story?
